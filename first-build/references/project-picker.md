# Picking the first project

## Ones that work, and why

| The project | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Rename 200 photos from the camera's numbering into a dated, readable pattern | The folder visibly changes. They can see instantly whether it is right. No account, no key. |
| Pull 5 figures out of a monthly PDF report into one row of a spreadsheet | They have done it by hand, so they can check it in seconds. The output is a file they already use. |
| Check a list of links and say which are dead | One command, a clear list at the end, and it answers a question they actually had. |
| Turn a messy client export into the format their template expects | Their own painful file, fixed. This one tends to produce the strongest reaction of any on the list. |
| Split one long document into a file per section | Visible, checkable, and the result is immediately useful. |
| Count how often each phrase appears across a folder of documents | Instant, and it usually shows them something they did not know. |

The pattern underneath all 6: a job they have done by hand, a result they can see, and nothing that needs a login.

## Ones that fail, and what to build instead

| What they ask for | Why it fails on day one | What to build today |
|---|---|---|
| "An app where my clients log in and see their reports" | Accounts, hosting, security, a database. Days of work before anything is visible. | Generate one client's report as a file, from their real numbers. |
| "Something that posts to my social accounts automatically" | Every platform needs an approved app and keys before a single line runs. | Write the week's posts into a dated file, ready to paste. |
| "A chatbot trained on my documents" | The interesting part is hidden, and they cannot tell whether an answer is right. | Search their documents for a phrase and show every match with its filename. |
| "A dashboard for my business" | Dashboard means 10 different things and none of them is one thing. | The single number they check most often, printed, from the real source. |
| "Automate my whole invoicing" | Money, a bank, a legal document. Wrong stakes for hour one. | Read last month's invoices and total them by client. |

The move in every row is the same: find the part with a visible result and no login, build that today, and write the rest into the README as the list for next time.

## When they insist on the big one

Do not fight it, and do not quietly substitute something smaller. Say that the big version is a real project worth doing, that it needs pieces that take longer than an hour, and that the fastest way to it is to have one piece working today. Then name the piece and start.

Almost everyone agrees once the smaller thing is named concretely. The disagreement is usually with the word "smaller", not with the actual project.
