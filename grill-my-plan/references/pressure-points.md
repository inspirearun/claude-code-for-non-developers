# Where each kind of plan tends to break

Read this when the plan fits one of the shapes below and the obvious questions have run out. These are starting points for real questions, so rewrite each one in the person's own situation before asking it. A question that names their tool, their client and their date gets a real answer. A generic one gets a generic answer.

## Building something

The usual break is that the thing gets built and then nobody uses it, including the person who asked for it.

- Who is the first person who will use this, by name, and have they said they want it?
- What are they doing today instead? Something is already solving this badly, and whatever it is, it is the real competition.
- What is the smallest version that would tell you whether this is worth finishing, and how long would that take?
- Once it exists, who fixes it when it breaks, and what happens the week they are away?
- What are you not doing while you build this?

## Buying a tool or subscribing to one

The break is usually that the tool is fine and it never gets adopted.

- What does this do that the tool you already pay for cannot?
- Who has to change how they work for this to pay off, and have they agreed to?
- What does it cost in a year, counting the setup time and the learning time, not just the monthly price?
- How would you get your data out if you left in 6 months?
- What happens when the free trial ends and it is only half set up?

## Hiring someone or bringing in help

The break is usually that the work needed defining more than it needed doing.

- Can you describe the first week's work in enough detail that someone could start on Monday?
- Who reviews what they produce, and how long will that take each week?
- What does it look like if it is not working, and how long would you give it?
- Is this work that repeats, or is it one job you are about to build a permanent arrangement around?

## Switching from one system to another

The break is usually the half-migrated state that lasts a year.

- What lives in the old system that has to come across, and have you looked at it recently, or are you remembering it?
- What is the plan for the period when both are running? That period is always longer than planned.
- What can the old one do that you have stopped noticing, and does the new one do it?
- What is the point of no return, and what happens if you find the problem after it?

## Launching or publishing

The break is usually that the date was chosen before the work was understood.

- What is the date driving from? A real external deadline, or a feeling that it should be soon?
- What is the one thing that, if it is not ready, means you do not go?
- Who sees it first, and what do you want them to say?
- What can you take out to make the date, and have you decided that now or will you decide it in a panic later?
- If nobody responds at all, what do you do next, and is that plan or hope?

## Taking on a commitment

The break is usually that the hours were never counted against the hours that already exist.

- How many hours a week, honestly, and where in the week are they coming from?
- How long does it run, and what does ending it early cost you?
- What have you already said yes to that runs over the same period?
- Who else is affected if this takes twice as long as you think, which it may well.

## The questions worth asking about any plan

Keep these in reserve. One of them usually opens something up when a grilling has gone flat.

- What would have to be true for this to be the wrong move? Someone who cannot answer at all has not examined it yet.
- What is the part you are least sure about? People almost always know, and they are almost always right.
- Who has done this before, and what happened to them?
- What is the reversible version of this decision, and why not do that first?
- If this fails, what will the reason turn out to have been? The honest answer to that is often already sitting in front of them.
