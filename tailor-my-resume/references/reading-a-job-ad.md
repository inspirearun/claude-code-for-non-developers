# Reading a job ad the way the person who wrote it did

A posting is a real description of a problem, wrapped in boilerplate that the company uses on every posting it publishes. The work is separating those two, and then working out which of the real parts carries weight.

## The passes

Read it 3 times. Each pass looks for one thing, because looking for all of them at once produces a summary rather than a target.

**Pass 1, the named things.** Every tool, system, platform, certification, method and metric that appears by name. Write them in a list, exactly as the posting spells them. HubSpot is not "marketing automation". Google Analytics 4 is not "analytics". The spelling matters because both the software and the person scanning are matching on the word.

**Pass 2, what repeats.** Anything appearing in more than one place is the job. A posting that mentions reporting in the summary, again under responsibilities and again under requirements is telling you what the person will do on Monday morning. Count it.

**Pass 3, the verbs.** Own, lead, define and set are a job where the person decides. Support, assist, execute and help are a job where somebody else decides. Coordinate sits in the middle and usually means responsibility without authority. This is the seniority signal, and it is more reliable than the title, which is inflated everywhere.

## Required against listed

Nearly every posting contains more requirements than the employer expects to find. Sort them into 3 buckets.

**Load bearing.** It is in the summary, or the first 3 responsibilities, or marked essential, or it repeats. Missing this is a real problem and you should say so.

**Genuinely wanted.** It appears once in the requirements list, stated plainly. Worth matching if it is true. Worth mentioning an adjacent version of if it is nearly true.

**Somebody's wish.** It sits in a list of 12 bullets, or under "nice to have", or it is the fourth programming language in a marketing role. Ignore it. A list like that is written by committee and nobody expects all of it.

## The problem behind the posting

Hiring starts because something hurts. Guess at what, out loud, and let the guess shape the ordering.

- A posting heavy on reporting and stakeholders is often a team that cannot explain its own results upward.
- A posting that mentions process, documentation or handover repeatedly has usually just lost somebody who held everything in their head.
- A posting that lists 3 tools by name and asks for migration experience is mid-migration and behind schedule.
- A brand new role in a small company means the person will build it from nothing, and evidence of working without structure matters more than any tool.

The guess is not always right. It still improves the resume, because it tells you which true thing to put first.

## A worked example

The posting, condensed:

> **Marketing Manager, B2B SaaS.** You will own our demand generation programme end to end. Reporting into the Head of Marketing, you will run campaigns across paid and email, own the monthly performance report for the leadership team, and work closely with sales on pipeline quality. We are looking for someone who can explain what is working and why.
>
> Requirements: 4+ years in B2B marketing. Hands on with HubSpot. Experience running paid social. Comfortable with reporting and attribution. Strong communication with senior stakeholders. Nice to have: SQL, Salesforce, experience in a company under 100 people.

**Named things:** HubSpot, paid social, email, attribution, Salesforce, SQL, B2B SaaS.

**What repeats:** reporting appears 3 times, in 3 different words, once as "explain what is working and why". Stakeholders and leadership appear twice. Reporting is the job.

**Verbs:** own, run, work closely with. They decide the programme but report into someone. Mid-level with real ownership.

**Buckets.** Load bearing: reporting and explaining results, HubSpot, demand generation. Genuinely wanted: paid social, email, working with sales. Somebody's wish: SQL, Salesforce, small company experience.

**The problem behind it:** leadership cannot see what marketing is producing, and the last person could not explain it. The candidate who wins is the one whose resume shows a monthly number going to a leadership team and a decision coming out of it.

**What that changes.** A candidate whose strongest work is campaign creative still leads with the reporting bullet, because that is what the posting is about. The creative work stays, lower down. Nothing is invented, and the order alone changes how the resume reads to the one person whose opinion decides.
